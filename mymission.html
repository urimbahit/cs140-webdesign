History of The PQCNM

In 1961 Missionary work officially began in the Philippines when the first four missionaries arrived in Pasay City. The Philippines Quezon City North Mission was officially established on July 1, 2011. The creation of the mission was announced by the Church in February 2011 to accommodate rapid member growth in the region. It was formed directly from a division of the Philippines Manila and Philippines Quezon City missions.

My Mission Journey

How it began
At first, I never had much of a motivation or desire to serve a mission, but out of reluctance and because I was always told to since a young age, I applied to serve a mission. I always saw people in my ward leave for their mission and once they came back, there was this change so drastic, I was so curious as to why is that. There was just something about their change that I wanted to have for myself as well. And that became my first motivation to serve a mission.

Before I started on my mission, or even receiving my call, I didn't really have a specific place I wanted to go. However, I was sure I did not want to go anywhere near home or the city, especially anywhere near Manila, the national capital region. As if the Lord was listening, I was called to serve in the Philippines Quezon City North, in which its boundaries encompass Manila and is literally a bus ride away from home. Embarrassingly enough, I wasn't as fluent in Tagalog as I am now, I mostly spoke English most of my life, so being sent to a Tagalog speaking area so close to home speaks volumes of how God truly listens to my prayers. I really didn't mind, but I was not ready for the unexpected experiences to come.

The experience
One of the crazier things I have experience in the mission was the environment. Most of my areas were a mix of a city and province, something I aws truly surprised to see. When I hearn I was sent to Quexon 'City' North, I expected a concrete jungle, but to my surprise, it was a mix of both. My areas were always densely packed with informal settlements, and most people that live there are from different parts of the Philippines. I was humbled every day I stepped outside. I saw a side of the Philippines I only often see on TV or documentaries. I was filled with gratitude for my situation and my upbringing, I was also filled with compassion as I served.

The environment they lived in also did not help. With no proper infrastructure or urban planning, often times when it rains, it floods. Even without the rain it would flood. As someone who has lived in the mountains, this was a first for me. Some areas I served in were flat and situated next t rivers. As the high tides come in, so does the flood. There was one occasion where we passed a road in the morning, no water to be seen, but as we exited the road in the evening, suddenly it had flooded three feet high.




The Promised Blessings
I have gained much during my time while serving a mission. As the parable of the talents commands, I multiplied my talents as much as I could. Eventually I was able to fluently speak Tagalog, I was able to speak confidently and even effectively teach the gospel in it. As someone who was mostly introverted, I gained extroverted skills, networking and communication skills I would never had gotten have I not served. I was also given an opportunity to serve in a leadership position; there I also gained adequate leadership skills supporting my fellow missionaries. Especially with missionaries hailing form different parts of the world. Another talent I was able to acquire, was the gift of music, though I was able to play a bit of instruments before, here I was able to learn the ukelele, piano and violin, though not that good, a talent nonetheless. I even tried recording my own ukelele cover of Gethsemene:



One of the other services I often did was tech support, often times there will be people we were teaching that had teachnology problems, I would always offer my help, I got pretty good at it too. And even while proselyting I would bring with me a small sketch pad and pen or an ‘EtchASketch’ and I would draw for people too. I great way for conversation and building trust and relationships.


Looking back, I never expected my mission to become as life changing as it is to me now. What started with reluctance became an experience that changed how I saw the people around me, my country, and myself. I gained talents, confidence, leadership and lifelong friends, but more importantly, I gained gratitude, compassion, and a stronger faith in God. I learned that sometimes the experiences we least expect can become the ones that shape us the most, and I am grateful that God allowed me such an opportunity to serve and grow in ways I never imagined.
